Friction Bloom

Controlled disruption. Observable structure.

Overview

Friction Bloom is an experimental algorithm for observing how systems respond when controlled disruption is introduced. It compares baseline behavior against altered behavior under constraint, then classifies the result as Bloom, Stable, Noise, or Collapse.

Bloom

Meaningful adaptation through a novel viable path.

High novelty with maintained coherence. The system adapts without collapse.

Stable

Behavior remains mostly unchanged under constraint.

Low novelty. Friction did not meaningfully alter the baseline path.

Noise

Behavior changes, but adaptation is weak or inefficient.

The agent reacts, but the altered route lacks strong adaptive value.

Collapse

The system fails to recover under pressure.

Utility recovery fails. Constraint exceeds adaptive capacity.

Dataset 001

The first 40-run map tested a Greedy Agent across scaled grid sizes with multiple friction types. Early findings showed that structural bottlenecks created stronger adaptation than scattered obstacles.

Replayability is the foundation for future observatory timelines, embeddings, semantic search, LLM training, behavioral clustering, and multi-agent analysis.

Without replay and state logging, the system only retains conclusions. With replay, it retains process.
